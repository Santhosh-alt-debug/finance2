How EMI is calculated, and why tenure matters more than you think

A longer tenure lowers your EMI but raises what you pay overall.

Read the full article

On a ₹5,00,000 loan at 10.5%, a 3-year tenure means an EMI of about ₹16,250 and roughly ₹85,000 in interest. Stretch it to 5 years and the EMI falls to about ₹10,750, but interest climbs to around ₹1.45 lakh.

Pick the shortest tenure your monthly budget can carry comfortably, and use the calculator to test two or three options before you apply.

Term plan or health plan first? A simple order

If you can only buy one this year, here is how to decide.

Read the full article

If anyone depends on your income, term life cover protects them from a sudden loss. If you have no health cover, one hospital stay can undo years of savings.

Most people need both. Start with health cover for the whole family, then add a term plan worth 10 to 15 times your annual income.

Investing · 30 Aug 2026

SIP basics: how ₹2,000 a month adds up

Small, regular investments can build real savings over time.

Read the full article

Investing ₹2,000 a month for 10 years at an assumed 12% a year would grow to about ₹4.6 lakh, of which ₹2.4 lakh is what you put in. Returns are not guaranteed and can be lower or negative.

The habit matters more than the amount. Automate the SIP on salary day and raise it by 10% each year.

Loans · 15 Aug 2026

Home loan: fixed or floating rate?

Understand the trade-off between certainty and savings.

Read the full article

A fixed rate keeps your EMI predictable but is usually higher. A floating rate moves with the market and often allows free part-payments.

If your budget is tight and you value certainty, consider fixed. If you plan to prepay, floating is usually the better fit.
